# The AMLA and EPPO Arrangement Connects Institutions, but Only Evidence Will Prove Cooperation

**SEO title:** AMLA and EPPO Cooperation: Governance, Controls and Operational Evidence  
**Meta description:** AMLA and the EPPO signed a Working Arrangement linking financial intelligence with prosecution. This analysis examines governance, controls, implementation dependencies and the evidence needed to demonstrate effective cooperation.  
**Publication date:** 18 November 2026  
**Category:** Strategic Perspectives & Industry Commentary  
**Publisher:** Chaintriviere Consulting

## Executive perspective

### Central thesis

The arrangement connects institutions, but only evidence will demonstrate effective operational cooperation.

### 1. Why the arrangement matters

The arrangement matters more for what it defers than what it grants. AMLA and the EPPO signed it on 17 November 2026 in Frankfurt. It links financial intelligence with criminal investigation at Union level.

Several operational details sit in later instruments absent from the published text. Organisations should therefore read it as direction, not as a control specification. In my assessment, the main implication concerns evidence quality across institutional boundaries.

## Institutional roles and cooperation framework

### 2. Mandates, legal basis and operational mechanisms

AMLA is the Union authority for anti-money laundering and countering terrorist financing. Its remit covers common rules and consistent supervision across the Union. Cooperation between national Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs) is part of its remit. The EPPO investigates and prosecutes crimes affecting the Union's financial interests. Article 2 frames cooperation around each party's mandate and relevant predicate offences.

Article 1 calls the arrangement technical, strategic and operational in nature. It has no legally binding effects on the Union or Member States. It also does not form the basis for exchanging personal data. Any exchange of personal data must therefore rely on an independent legal basis; Article 17 governs the protection of personal data exchanged under the arrangement.

The AMLA Regulation is Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 of 31 May 2024. The EPPO Regulation is Council Regulation (EU) 2017/1939 of 12 October 2017. Article 23 brings the arrangement into force on the date of signature.

Operational cooperation covers information exchange, joint analyses and case coordination. Exchanged material includes case-related financial intelligence, transaction data and investigative findings. AMLA must report criminal conduct within EPPO competence without undue delay.

The EPPO informs AMLA of the steps taken in relation to information received, and any relevant outcomes, to the extent permitted by its legal framework. Separately, requests for information must be answered expeditiously and, unless otherwise stipulated, within 20 working days. Article 5 also provides for implementing technical standards on the reporting format.

The EPPO may ask AMLA to assess whether to initiate joint analyses. EPPO participation in AMLA-coordinated joint analyses requires the participating FIUs' consent. Article 3(e) lists implementing a hit/no-hit system under Article 40(8) AMLA Regulation. Its methods and procedures are left to a subsequent instrument. Recital 45 of that Regulation conditions such matching on participating FIUs' consent.

The parties may also exchange typologies, risk indicators and money laundering methodologies. The EPPO joins AMLA working groups drafting technical standards and guidelines. It observes, without voting, FIU Board meetings addressing matters within its mandate.

Training and staff exchanges are subject to the availability of resources. Seconded liaison officers would require a separate instrument setting detailed terms. The source establishes these provisions, but not their operational performance.

## The institutional and operational chain

### 3. The underlying structural change

The structural change is a connected chain from FIU analysis to prosecution. Each institution keeps its own mandate, legal framework and independent decision-making powers. FIU consent remains a gate, which preserves existing accountability lines. Article 7 asks parties to consider informing each other of overlapping proceedings. Consultation aims to avoid duplication and respect *ne bis in idem*. In my assessment, upstream financial intelligence may increasingly inform downstream criminal proceedings.

Article 6 asks where AMLA has reported information to the EPPO but the EPPO has not yet decided whether to open an investigation, AMLA shall, where appropriate, avoid activities that may compromise potential evidence and consult the EPPO before taking precautionary measures, unless immediate action is required.

Articles 8 and 9 address EPPO requests concerning AMLA itself. Article 8 covers requests to lift privileges or immunities, while Article 9 concerns the inviolability of AMLA's premises, buildings and archives. This inward-facing dimension means that the Arrangement also addresses investigative access to the authority itself.

The operative text does not impose duties on obliged entities directly. Any effect on obliged entities remains my interpretation, not an official statement.

### 4. Operational and technology implications

For case-specific exchanges, Article 4 requires respect for marking, classification and handling. Article 4 asks for secure, encrypted channels to the extent possible. Article 5 makes secure, encrypted channels mandatory for reporting and subsequent exchanges. Article 15 says channel implementation may be agreed in a separate instrument.

The arrangement does not specify interfaces, system configurations or data formats. My reading is that hit/no-hit matching implies data standards and access controls. Architecture decisions should therefore await sufficiently detailed specifications from the later instruments.

Contact points sit in operational units, suggesting operational ownership of workflows. In my view, analysts, legal teams and system owners must coordinate workflows.

## Information governance and traceability

### 5. Data protection, information handling and traceability

Each party applies its own data protection framework to exchanged personal data. Parties must inform each other of breaches and consult on data-subject requests. Parties inform each other where data appear inaccurate, incomplete or wrongly transmitted. Personal data must be processed only for the purpose for which it was transmitted, must not be processed incompatibly with that purpose, and must not be stored longer than necessary.

These provisions suggest several information-governance considerations, offered here as analysis only:

- Information provenance should remain traceable throughout subsequent exchanges between the institutions.
- Access permissions should reflect institutional responsibilities and applicable use restrictions.
- Correction requests should generate documented and auditable records of corrective action.
- Retention decisions should remain demonstrable throughout the complete information lifecycle.
- Transmission records should preserve classification and handling information for later review.

These are assurance considerations, not additional contractual or legal requirements.

### 6. Technical standardisation and dependencies

Standardised reporting can support consistency, but cannot guarantee interoperability or accuracy. Article 41(2) of the AMLA Regulation provides the basis for those standards. The hit/no-hit mechanism adds another dependency on later detailed specification.

## Strategic considerations for organisations

### 7. Control design, oversight and accountability

The arrangement shows identifiable control-design elements: contact points, deadlines and templates. Article 14 designates permanent contact points, notably the two operational unit heads. Article 19 escalates unresolved disagreements to the Chief Prosecutor and AMLA Chair. Article 12 provides for regular meetings and possible reciprocal leadership invitations. These mechanisms support ownership, but cannot demonstrate operational effectiveness by themselves.

Article 16 requires mutual consultation before media communication on jointly involved matters. Replies to such consultation requests are preferably given within 48 hours. Together these provisions cover communication risk as well as information risk.

#### Control design versus operating effectiveness

Control design is visible through defined responsibilities, procedures and safeguards. Operating effectiveness needs evidence of execution, which a signed text cannot provide. In practice, assurance would examine operational records and documented outcomes.

Possible evidence includes the following examples, which are illustrative rather than mandatory:

- Information requests, responses and timestamps show whether reply deadlines were met.
- Outstanding requests should carry documented reasons for any delay or escalation.
- Provenance and correction records show how inaccurate information was identified and handled.
- Access permissions and handling restrictions should be recorded and periodically reviewed.
- Retention and deletion decisions should be supported by dated, reviewable evidence.
- Records of consultations and escalations show how overlapping actions were coordinated.

#### Information accuracy and liability

Article 20 makes information accuracy a liability consideration for each party. Each party answers for damage caused to an individual under its framework. Neither party may plead the other's inaccurate information against an injured individual.

Where AMLA has reported information to the EPPO but the EPPO has not yet decided whether to open an investigation, AMLA shall, where appropriate, avoid activities that may compromise potential evidence and consult the EPPO before taking precautionary measures, unless immediate action is required.

Management would need data on request volumes, delays and outcomes for oversight. Sustainable cooperation also depends on people, reflected in training and liaison provisions.

### 8. Implications for obliged entities, vendors and assurance functions

The arrangement creates no new direct obligations for obliged entities or vendors. Its immediate scope concerns cooperation between AMLA and the EPPO. Organisations should still monitor institutional developments and forthcoming technical standards and guidelines.

#### Obliged entities and financial institutions

Existing AML obligations remain governed by applicable legislation, not by this arrangement. Reliable reporting and transaction documentation remain sensible practice for any organisation.

#### RegTech and enterprise software providers

Vendors should distinguish published requirements from implementation assumptions in product planning. In my view, audit trails and export capabilities are no-regret investments. Data lineage can strengthen traceability across integrations, as a design option only.

Premature architecture commitments could create avoidable cost before specifications are published. Sequencing matters because later instruments will determine what systems must actually support. Early work should therefore focus on evidence, documentation and data quality foundations.

#### Compliance, risk and internal audit

Compliance teams can monitor guidance while risk functions assess information-sharing dependencies. Internal audit can examine supporting governance and control arrangements where relevant. Three considerations deserve evaluation, none of them mandated by the arrangement:

1. First, test whether case documentation could withstand prosecutorial scrutiny, beyond supervisory review.
2. Second, map data lineage so transaction evidence stays traceable across systems.
3. Third, watch the hit/no-hit instrument before committing architecture or roadmap resources.

These activities should remain proportionate to exposure and imply no new obligations. Resource allocation should reflect each organisation's actual role in reporting chains.

## Limitations and final perspective

### 9. Limitations and uncertainties

The published text provides commitments, not demonstrated outcomes or performance measurements. The source gives no evidence on request volumes, timelines or enforcement outcomes. Operative articles of the underlying legislation should be verified against EUR-Lex.

- First, hit/no-hit methods and procedures require a subsequent instrument under Article 3(e).
- Second, secure channels may be agreed separately, so full architecture remains unspecified.
- Third, secondment terms for liaison officers depend on a separate instrument.
- Fourth, Article 22 permits termination in writing with three months' notice.

Following termination, the parties must agree on the continued use and storage of information already exchanged. Confidentiality obligations survive termination, as do applicable legal obligations concerning cooperation and information exchange.

### 10. Final perspective

The arrangement connects institutions, but connection alone does not prove cooperation. Governance sets responsibilities, while control assurance tests whether they operate effectively. Information quality links both dimensions, because accountability requires traceable records.

Its value will depend on instruments, records and behaviour that follow signature. Governance quality, not signature alone, will determine whether the chain works.

### 11. Key takeaways

1. The arrangement is non-binding and does not authorise personal data exchange.
2. Hit/no-hit methods await a later instrument, and channel implementation may follow separately.
3. Evidence quality, data lineage and timeliness records are the practical readiness indicators.
4. Obliged entities gain no direct duties here, yet should monitor forthcoming standards.
5. Linking intelligence to prosecution is easy to sign and hard to evidence.
